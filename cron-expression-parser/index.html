Cron Format

A standard cron expression has 5 fields separated by spaces: minute hour day-of-month month day-of-week. Each field accepts numbers, ranges (1-5), lists (1,3,5), steps (*/5), and the wildcard *.

Field Values

Minute: 0–59
Hour: 0–23
Day of month: 1–31
Month: 1–12 (or JAN–DEC)
Day of week: 0–6 (0 = Sunday, or SUN–SAT)

Special Characters

* — any value
, — list separator (e.g., 1,15,30)
- — range (e.g., 1-5)
/ — step (e.g., */5 means every 5)

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "*/5 * * * *" mean?

Every 5 minutes. The first field (*/5) steps through minute values 0, 5, 10, 15, etc. The remaining fields are wildcards.

What's the difference between 5-field and 6-field cron?

Standard Unix cron uses 5 fields (no seconds). Some systems (Quartz, AWS) use 6 or 7 fields including seconds and years. This tool parses the standard 5-field format.

How do I run a job at midnight on the 1st of every month?

0 0 1 * * — minute 0, hour 0, day 1, any month, any weekday.

Do you log my cron expressions?

No. Parsing happens in your browser. Nothing is sent to our servers.
