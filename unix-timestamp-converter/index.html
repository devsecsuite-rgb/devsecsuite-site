What Is a Unix Timestamp?

A Unix timestamp is the number of seconds that have elapsed since January 1, 1970 at 00:00:00 UTC (the "Unix epoch"). It's the standard way computers represent time because it's a single integer that works across every time zone and platform.

Seconds vs Milliseconds

Unix timestamps are traditionally in seconds. JavaScript and some APIs use milliseconds. A 10-digit number is seconds (e.g., 1758027600). A 13-digit number is milliseconds (e.g., 1758027600000). This tool detects both automatically.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my timestamp show a different date?

Unix timestamps are always in UTC. Your local time zone offset is applied when displayed. The "Local Time" field shows what your device thinks the time is.

What's the year 2038 problem?

Older 32-bit systems store Unix timestamps as signed 32-bit integers, which overflow on January 19, 2038. Modern 64-bit systems handle dates far beyond this.

Do you store my timestamps?

No. Everything runs in your browser using JavaScript. No data is ever sent to our servers.
